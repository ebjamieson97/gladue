What are Gladue factors?

When a court sentences an Indigenous person in Canada, it must consider the unique systemic and background factors that may have played a part in bringing that person before the court. These are known as Gladue factors, after the Supreme Court of Canada's 1999 decision in R. v. Gladue.

They include the effects of colonialism, residential schools, and loss of culture and language, as well as other similar factors. Courts must also consider sentencing options that are deemed culturally appropriate, including restorative justice. Since 2021, courts have applied similar reasoning to anti-Black racism.

"All available sanctions, other than imprisonment, that are reasonable in the circumstances and consistent with the harm done to victims or to the community should be considered for all offenders, with particular attention to the circumstances of Aboriginal offenders." Criminal Code, s. 718.2(e)
